The three round-11 findings in plain words

FindingRiskWhat was done
An account switch erased another account's data. This was my decision and it was wrong.When account A signed out and B signed in, A's whole journal was wiped: sessions, key, quarantine. GPS tracks and heart rate not yet uploaded were lost for good.Each account now has its own journal and its own encryption key. Sign-out and account switch erase nothing. Only the deleted account's journal is wiped, and only that one.
A lost owner marker let the next account adopt another account's journal.The journal was tied to an account by a separate flag in preferences. Without the flag a new account could get foreign data.The flag is gone. Ownership is the location of the data: the directory and the key are named by a hash of the account id, so nothing can be swapped.
Bulk local deletions were not guaranteed to reach the cloud.Deletions were pushed 8 per pass with no continuation, and a daily limit of 100 was shared with ordinary calls. Thirty offline deletions needed four app starts.The pass continues in rounds while there is something to send (up to 1024 deletions at once). The remainder is retried with growing delays, then a slow fallback timer. A separate limit of 1000 deletions per day. The count of not-yet-sent deletions is exposed in the state.

What the second independent review found in the fixes themselves

ProblemRiskWhat was done
A sync pass of account A that was running at the moment of an account switch kept working on account B. All four reviewers found it.B's entries were deleted using A's deletion list. A's data could be written to the cloud under B. Activity ids coincide across accounts when the same activity was imported.Every operation remembers the account it started under and re-checks it before every write and every cloud call. When the cloud user and the journal point to different accounts, the operation does nothing.
An unbound journal answered "empty". The screen showed "all in sync" and tapping "keep mine" ended in "success".The user saw success although nothing happened.It is now a visible error with a retry button. Sync and conflict resolution do not pretend to succeed in that state.
A live conflict screen kept the previous account's data after a switch.The new account saw foreign values.The screen refreshes on account change and when the journal binds.
The deletion queue state lived for the whole app run.A's timer could fire a push under B, and a deletion confirmed for A would not be pushed for B.The state is reset on sign-out and account change.
The retry counter was never reset.After about 44 minutes offline there were no more retries until the app restarted.The counter resets after a clean pass, and after six attempts one fallback timer fires hourly.
A wiped account could come back as an empty scope with a new key on a repeated sign-in event.Leftovers of a deleted account on the device.Re-binding a wiped account is refused until sign-out or another account.
The encryption key could remain after a wipe that reported success.A false "device is clean".A failed key delete now fails the wipe, the pending marker stays and the next launch retries.
The stream uploader state survived an account switch.The new account's upload could fail to start in that app run.The state is reset on account change.
One cloud-upload consent cache shared by all accounts (external reviewer, round 12). Account A's startup chain, after a long sync, wrote A's consent when B was already current.B's GPS data could be uploaded on A's consent. The server checks consent separately, so the reviewer did not establish unauthorized server storage, but the on-phone rule "consent belongs to the same account" was broken.Consent is bound to the account: the store knows whose uid it holds, an obsolete sign-in cannot overwrite state, uploading requires the consent account to equal the upload account, the startup chain re-verifies the account after every step, and the consent store is bound at the very start. Stored consent of every account is kept.
Siblings of that defect found by a sweep of the whole surface: grant and withdraw applied their result to the wrong account, the "delete cloud streams requested" flag was shared by everyone, a tap queued under A ran under B, the conflict screen showed the previous account's cards, sync and uploads ran during account deletion, the sign-out wipe and the export were not bound to the account.Showing or acting on another account's data, a silent refusal for the second account, a false "deleted".Everything is bound to the account and covered by tests with deterministic interleavings. The window while the server deletes the account is closed by the fence in the database rules (verified in firestore.rules).
The new code contained a compile error: a type check did not behave as assumed. The app did not build.The app does not build.Fixed. The first test run found it, not the reviewers and not the implementer.

What I missed, in plain words

  • I decided to wipe the journal on an account switch, checked only the leak risk between accounts and not what is lost. I also told two reviewers not to re-check that code, and they approved it. Only the external reviewer caught it.
  • The fix of that decision had defects of its own that the first tests did not look for: they tested an account switch between operations, not in the middle of a running one. The second independent review found that.
  • I did not put the consent cache under review because I limited the reviewers to the changed code. A defect of the same class lived next door and only the external reviewer found it. Now, after a defect class is found, I sweep the whole surface for it, not just the diff.
  • The project now keeps a lessons log, core/LESSONS_LOG.md, with an entry in every commit, including my own mistakes. The sprint retrospective is built from it.

  • Accepted limits, not defects. (1) Device data of an anonymous account stays after sign-out: wiping it would repeat the rejected data loss, so it is handled with the sign-out export offer in S03. (2) The user-facing "N deletions not yet synced" notice belongs to the S03 screens, the state for it already exists. (3) After a failed journal bind, sync resumes at the next sign-in event or restart; nothing is lost and nothing leaks. (4) A tap during an account switch does nothing; the next refresh shows the new account. (5) The export does not notice an A, B, A switch within one read (no caller yet). (6) If the sign-out wipe fails after wiping, that account is not re-bound until sign-out or another account. (7) One widget control in the tests hung for no known reason and was replaced by a presenter test.
