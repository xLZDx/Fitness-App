The four survivors

A killed mutation confirms a guard. A survivor teaches you something you did not know. All four here were the same species: an assertion satisfied by a wrong implementation.

1 · The fixture that scaled with the constant it bounded

The metric locator's proximity window was widened from 120 to 100,000 and the suite stayed green — because the test built its own gap as WINDOW * 3. The fixture moved with the thing it was supposed to pin. Rewritten with a literal 4,000-character gap and a bounds assertion.

2 · The catch nothing could enter

A failure policy was mutated to swallow every quota refusal, and nothing went red. The catch sat around a live Firebase call, so no test could reach it and no mutation inside it could ever die. Fixed structurally rather than by adding a test: the policy moved into a method any throwing closure can enter. The mutation then died.

3 · The guard that counted numerals

A provenance document must not reconcile a 29-class training log with a 37-label artefact — no evidence connects them. The guard was "29" in doc and "37" in doc. Rewriting the document to say the log was 37-class — the exact falsification the guard exists to prevent — left it green, because both numerals still appeared elsewhere in the file.

4 · The repair that was still too weak

Fixed to assert the assignment, it was then mutated again: the inventory row flipped to "29 entries" while the prose two sections down still said 37. any(line says 37) was satisfied by a different line than the one falsified. An internally inconsistent record is not a preserved contradiction — it is a broken document. Both directions now asserted for both files.

The gate that moved money

A guest account could buy a subscription it could never cancel

startFreeTrial refused anonymous callers; createCheckoutSession did not. The guard sat on the cheaper of the two doors. This was already on file as an open finding left to the operator, on the grounds that both candidate fixes were product decisions.

Two independent reviews, run separately and never shown each other's output, produced two facts that were not available when that was written. First: the subscription is not merely unrecoverable, it is uncancellable — the billing portal, account deletion and receipts all key off a uid an anonymous user cannot sign back into. Reinstall the app and the card keeps being charged with no route in the product to stop it. lifetime was purchasable this way. Second: the alternative fix is affirmatively unsafe — exempting subscribers from the anonymous quota divisor makes one Stripe authorisation, on a stolen card, enough to sweep the licensed library in three days, inside the chargeback window.

That moved it from a revenue preference to a correctness fix. Four lines, reverted by deletion, imposing exactly the friction the free trial already imposes. Five tests assert on the Stripe mock rather than only the thrown code — a guard that throws after creating the customer has prevented nothing. Four mutations killed, including one written as a google.com allow-list, which locks every email/password payer out of paying.
