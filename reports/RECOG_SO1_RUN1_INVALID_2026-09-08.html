The arithmetic that settles it

From the run's own usage blocks: the mean prompt is 2,153 tokens and the maximum 2,198 (arm A 2,113, arm B 2,198). I planned against 1,813. An 18% underestimate, and it was mine — not a change in the provider's behaviour.

A clean 104-observation pass, with zero retries, costs roughly 2,153 × 104 = 223,962 input tokens against a 200,000 daily ceiling. The experiment does not fit into one day on this tier at all. The retry policy made it worse — 260 requests for 104 observations — but even a flawless run is 112% of a day.

A defect in the sealed pre-registration itself

The power floor was meant to be the guard against exactly this: a degraded run must not produce a verdict. It does not do that.

unique_sources in scripts/dev/recog_so1_score.py counts rows, not answers. For this run it reports 26/26/26/26 and passes: all 104 rows exist — 58 of them simply have no reply. The guard's description is broader than the check behind it.

The scorer was not edited. Changing a sealed scorer once a run exists is precisely what the seal forbids. The defect is recorded and goes into the revision, not into a quiet patch.

GPT-PM's ruling, adopted in full

  • Option A. The run is an invalid instrument, preserved unscored; a new pre-registration revision and a clean re-run follow. Changing provider was rejected — it would change the hypothesis while Groq remains feasible across two days. Scoring what exists was rejected.
  • A run-validity gate before scoring, separating INVALID_INSTRUMENT (a provider, configuration or quota failure makes the measurement structurally invalid — no statistics produced) from INCONCLUSIVE (execution valid, too few valid answers survived).
  • An answer-coverage floor: at least 20 unique source photographs per ground-truth kind carrying a valid parsed answer in both arms. A 429, timeout, 5xx or parse failure does not count toward it merely because its manifest row exists.
  • The re-run is two deterministic day partitions, frozen before transmission: 13 + 13 sources per day, both arms of a source always on the same day, so day and quota conditions cannot confound ground-truth kind or arm.
  • Retry policy by quota class, not by status code. TPD exhaustion stops that day's partition; an OTPM refusal is an instrument failure and also stops; ordinary ITPM honours Retry-After with a tight bound. No cycling of keys or accounts to evade an organization limit.

Consent: GPT-PM read the record more strictly than I did

I proposed that the committed consent covers a re-run — the same photographs, the same endpoint, the same retention state. GPT-PM disagreed, and is right: the record authorised that manifest once, and the completed run consumed that authorisation. Identical files and endpoint reduce the incremental privacy change; they do not turn "once" into "as many attempts as it takes".

A second transmission requires a new explicit operator reaffirmation — recording that the first 104-observation transmission occurred and failed as an instrument, that a second pass over the same 52 photographs to the same frozen model is proposed, that it will span several days, and that Global ZDR remains enabled. That decision belongs to the operator and to nobody else.
