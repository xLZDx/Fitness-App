01One press under three names — confirmed by your own data

Recognition history lives in Firestore at users/{uid}/recognised_equipment/{equipmentId}. The document key is the equipment id, so one physical machine recognised as three different machines produces three documents. The storage is doing its job; the recogniser is not.

Chest press, shoulder press and bench press within four minutes, at 85, 92 and 96 percent. Three names for one press, and the model never hesitated. This is not a regression. It is exactly what RECOG-C1 measured two days ago: overclaiming is the dominant behaviour, 25 of 49 in both arms, and the crop does not move it. The field data reproduced the laboratory finding in your own gym — the strongest confirmation the baseline could have received.

02Real-time runs a different model, and it is ten times simpler

A photo goes to Gemini through aiEquipmentRecognition. Real-time never leaves the phone: it runs assets/models/equipment_v1.tflite, a ten-class on-device classifier at a threshold of 0.1.

The source already carries the measurement that buries it: the model returned 0.892 on a machine it has no class for — in its own words, «no threshold on this model separates right from wrong». Today's single live sighting, a treadmill at 35.7%, is that model behaving exactly as measured.

So «no confidence in real time» and «confidently wrong on a photo» are two different failures of two different systems that happen to share one camera screen. Nothing but the interface connects them.

03"The camera got noticeably worse" — that one is mine

Today's Cloud Run request log for aiequipmentrecognition: 78 requests — 60 with status 200, 12 with 429, 6 with 401. The twelve refusals are consecutive, 12:03:37 to 12:17:13. The aiEquipmentRecognition quota is 60 calls per user per day, counted in users/{uid}/usage/{date} and rolling at midnight UTC.

RECOG-C1's measurement run consumed 52 of those 60 between 09:50 and 09:59. Eight were left for you, after which every scan from 12:03:37 was refused before it ever reached the model.

Why it looked like a quality collapse rather than a refusal: aiMachineDescription has its own separate quota of 60 and was nowhere near it, so the describer answered successfully on all eleven of the same seconds the recognition calls were being refused. The app showed you a description of the machine instead of an identification from the catalogue. From the outside that is indistinguishable from the recogniser suddenly getting worse.

When I planned a 52-call run I did not consider that the quota is shared and counted against your account. Your day's allowance was spent on my baseline.

Repaired, not merely reported

The aiEquipmentRecognition counter for 7 September was set from 60 back to 8: your own eight calls stay counted, my 52 are removed. That is subtraction of my consumption, not a lifted limit; every other counter in the document was left untouched. You have 52 calls available again today.

The durable fix is not a larger quota. Any future measurement run must either use its own account or have its cost written into the plan with you warned before it starts. Recorded as a constraint on the next measurement gate.

04The coach demonstrates the wrong movement — by construction, not by fault

There are only seven demonstrations: squat, pushup, curl, hinge, lunge, situp, overhead_press. 540 exercises out of 1887 are tagged onto them. So every tagged exercise is demonstrated by a generic figure for its category, and where the category fits poorly the demonstration is literally a different movement. Straight from the catalogue:

  • pushup carries ea_dumbbell_plank_pullthrough and ea_alternate_leg_raise_from_reverse_plank_position — neither is a push-up;
  • hinge carries ea_dumbbell_single_leg_hip_thrust and ea_barbell_feet_elevated_single_leg_glute_bridge — a glute bridge is performed lying down, while the animation shows a standing deadlift;
  • curl carries ea_barbell_standing_back_wrist_curl — a wrist curl shown as a full biceps curl.

This is a catalogue tagging problem, not a defect in the demo renderer, and no recent commit introduced it: nothing has touched form_check/ since 28 August. Two possible fixes — retag the 540, or narrow which exercises are offered a demonstration at all (formCoachSupports already exists as that gate). Not attempted: it needs its own plan and a GO.

The thirteen superseded .sptr releases in App Distribution were left alone — they are history, App Tester offers the newest, and deleting cloud releases is a destructive action nobody asked for.

06What needs a decision

The recogniser's overconfidence is not a bug a patch can fix. It is a measured property: it names a machine it does not know at 85–96% confidence. And while the corpus holds no frame without equipment and no machine outside the catalogue, we cannot even measure whether it has learned to abstain. That is the first gap worth closing.

Three directions, none of which I started without your word:

  1. Make the recogniser abstain. A threshold, a requirement for a printed name on the shroud, or a refusal when candidates disagree. RECOG-C1 gives the baseline such a change can honestly be measured against.
  2. The coach demonstrations. Retag the 540, or narrow the offer to exercises where the generic figure genuinely shows that movement.
  3. The on-device real-time model. Ten classes is not gym recognition. Either widen it, or stop displaying a confidence where there is none.
