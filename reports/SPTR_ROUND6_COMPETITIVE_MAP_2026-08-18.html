02

A correction round four owes about its own product

The nameplate does not identify the machine model

Round four implied that reading the shroud gives model-level knowledge. It does not, and the code is explicit about why: the anchor deliberately discards brand words — NAUTILUS, STAR TRAC, INSPIRATION — because they appear on half the machines in a gym and identify nothing. What the anchor reads is the exercise name printed on the shroud, which resolves to a catalog category.

Confirmed in the data: all 69 equipment records carry manufacturer: "Any". There is no model dimension at all. Liftd holds 1,300+ models across 50+ brands.

Consequence: any positioning built on "it knows exactly which machine you are standing at" is untrue today and expensive to make true. Model resolution is a data-acquisition project, not a feature.

03

The candidate nobody had, found by looking closer at what already ships

Auditable identification

The scanner already prints the exact words it read off the machine, under the answer and the confidence: "Read on the machine: ABDUCTION ADDUCTION" / «Прочитано на тренажёре: ABDUCTION ADDUCTION». Both languages ship; it renders in scanner_page.dart:1349.

So the identification is checkable by the user in one second, against the object in front of them. Every competitor asserts a result — RALLY, Gymeo, GymScan, Fitzi all hand back a name and ask for trust. Liftd's QR is verifiable but needs the gym to install it.

The supporting behaviour is unusually disciplined and also already built: confidence is capped at 0.92 and never reaches 1.0, because "a confidence the model did not produce must not be shown"; two machine names in the text return two candidates at 0.55 rather than a choice; three or more return the cable station at 0.75 instead of one of its six exercises.

A targeted search found no product claiming this. Stated carefully: no product found claiming it — not proof that none exists.

This matters beyond novelty. It is the one form of "show what you know, show when you don't" that a stranger understands in a second and can test immediately, without reading a methodology page or trusting a percentage. It converts the brand principle from a claim into a visible object.

05

The two hostile probes

Contribution loop — verdict OPEN, medium-high confidence

No fitness product was found running the full cycle. The closed-catalog recognisers — GymScan, GymLens, Gymeo, Exercaise, PhotoFit — have no unknown-scan submission, no moderation queue and no "we'll add it" promise anywhere in their copy. Their failure mode is the nearest-wrong-answer one this product is trying to displace.

Outside fitness the pattern is mature and worth borrowing deliberately: Seek pushes images its model cannot identify into iNaturalist, the community identifies them, and those identifications retrain the model. Open Food Facts turns a missing barcode into a user photograph that enters a public database — structurally the closest analogue, because the objects are bounded and physical and the entry is immediately useful to the next person in the same shop, or the same gym chain.

The honest kill argument: this is not one moat but six commodity steps, and it only compounds if repeat-sighting density in one market actually accumulates. That is an operations bet on content throughput, not a technology bet — and a funded incumbent can buy the same density by paying gym chains for equipment lists.

Uncertainty as a system — verdict PARTIALLY OCCUPIED

The promise is already claimed in nutrition. Honest Calories markets refusal of false precision as the product and cites a published tolerance as its gate; PlateLens applies the same posture to two distinct surfaces. Neither withholds output — both widen a number into a range.

In fitness, nothing occupies it: every rep counter found markets confident automatic counting.

The caution worth carrying, and it is uncomfortable: showing a range is friendlier to a user than showing nothing. The version that sounds more defensible may be the commercially weaker one. That is an argument for pairing every refusal with something the user still gets — which is exactly what the shipped rep-count badge already does when it says the count is off but the form check is still running.

One more finding arrived independently and is worth stating on its own, because it was reached by a different reviewer looking at a different question: not one of the seven camera-scan products documents what happens when recognition fails, shows a confidence level, withholds an uncertain answer, or offers a contribution path. Silent-wrong-answer is the category default. That is the same conclusion section 03 reached from the code, from the opposite direction.

05

The pattern six rounds have been circling

Read the three open rows together. Showing the evidence instead of the answer. Withholding a number that cannot be stood behind. Admitting a miss and turning it into content. None of them is a capability. All three are refusals — and all three are cheap to build and awkward to copy for exactly the same reason: they subtract from the pitch every competitor is already making.

Every feature in this category can be copied in a sprint. A refusal cannot be copied without deleting a competitor's own demo. The finding, stated once

This also resolves the tension that has run through rounds three to five. The counter-argument to restraint-as-positioning was always that nobody installs an app to be told it is unsure — which is correct, and is why restraint keeps failing as a hero line. But auditable identification is not a confession. It is a feature with a visible surface: the machine says ABDUCTION ADDUCTION, the app says it read ABDUCTION ADDUCTION, and the user's own eyes close the loop. That is restraint in a form a stranger can want.

06

The bundle, judged honestly

Scan it · Set it · Remember it is the right product architecture and the wrong brand. Every component is now demonstrably held by someone: RALLY has scan-and-remember without infrastructure, Liftd has it with, GymSetup has set. No single product was found holding all three, camera-only, in Russian — but a bundle assembled from commodities answers "why this app" and not "why remember this app". It is a roadmap and a comparison-table argument, not a position.

Where it does earn its place is as the order of the page and the order of the build: it is a coherent sequence, each step creating the reason for the next, and it absorbs future machine-level features without renaming anything.

The one component worth protecting

Camera-only is the constraint doing the real work. Liftd, WeightFlow, Setto and LIFTAG all need a sticker, a QR, an NFC tag or a gym partnership — which means none of them can serve a person walking into an arbitrary gym today. RALLY can, and is the genuine competitor. Any positioning here should carry "no sticker, no setup, no permission from the gym" as an explicit clause, because it silently excludes half the field.

The competitor that should worry you most

RYZN. Shipping on iOS and Apple Watch, monetised, updated in July: it reads the machine's label to disambiguate and auto-cascades your last performance. That is nameplate OCR plus machine memory in one product — two of round four's three top territories, already live. It has no Russian, no setup guidance, and no failure handling, which is precisely where the remaining room is.

Second is GymVision on a different axis: it films the room and builds the gym profile automatically, with a shared gym map. Territory 07 is not partly open; it is taken.

And a note that cuts the other way: GymScan, whose headline the concept collided with, is a waitlist. Version 1.1, stale since September, no displayed ratings, no ship date. The sentence is occupied; the competitor behind it is not a threat.

08

Next gate

No winner is crowned, per round five's instruction. Two measurements and one search decide it, and none of them is a landing page.

  1. Nameplate field study. Across several gyms and brands: what fraction of machines carry a readable name, in what language, and what the anchor scores end to end on those photographs. This unblocks the cross-language concept and retires the two stale recognition figures still circulating from earlier rounds.
  2. The contribution-loop search, now running as a hostile probe against fitness and against the identification apps outside fitness that are famous for this pattern.
  3. A concept test, not a launch. Two hooks, one page each, same product footage: the auditable-identification line against the private-confidence line. That is a cheap answer to the one question this review genuinely cannot settle from evidence — which of a memorable mechanism and a felt emotion moves a stranger further.
Adopted for every future round

Round five's competitive rule is now standing procedure: search the user job and its synonyms rather than the feature name; treat QR, NFC and camera implementations as separate searches; search one-feature indie apps rather than category leaders; search in English and in Russian; and record which of the four collision types applies. "Fitbod does not do it" is not evidence, and this review produced that error twice before it was caught by someone else.
