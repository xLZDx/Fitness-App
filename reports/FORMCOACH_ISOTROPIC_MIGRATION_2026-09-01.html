What was wrong

The pose detector divides both axes by the frame's height. So y runs 0 to 1 and x runs 0 to the aspect ratio. The authored targets were written the other way: x as a fraction of width. Two places reconciled that by multiplying by the aspect ratio.

Multiplying one axis is an anisotropic scale, and poseMatchScore only survives isotropic ones — it measures shape through angles, and angles do not survive squeezing an image along a single axis. The consequence is measurable: the same physical pose scored 0.1366 apart between a 9:16 and a 3:4 camera. That is 22.8% of 0.6, the mean joint offset at which the score reaches zero.

What shipped

  • x_new = x_old × 2/3 across all 84 joints of all 14 targets. 2/3 is the reference phone's own aspect ratio, so behaviour there is preserved.
  • The aspect-ratio multiplication deleted from poseMatchScore and debugMatchBreakdown; the xScale parameter deleted from buildSilhouette along with its call site.
  • Invariance asserted for all 14 targets across 9:16, 2:3, 3:4 and 1:1 frames at 1e-9, plus a negative control — so invariance was not bought by making the comparison agree with everything.

Equivalent, not identical. I first wrote that the change was "exactly nil" on the reference device. GPT-PM rejected that, correctly: the constants are rounded to three decimals, and 0.47 × 2/3 is 0.313333…, not 0.313. The real figures are measured and pinned by tests — the worst coordinate lands 3.33e-4 from the exact quotient, and a body standing in the exact pre-migration shape scores 2.96e-3 short of 1.0. Against a 0.80 pass mark that is indistinguishable, but it is not zero, and a durable record should carry the honest number.

Defect A: the silhouette ran off the screen

While x was being rescaled to the frame, the figure was automatically squeezed to fit a narrow screen. With the rescale gone, three silhouettes no longer fitted a 9:16 frame (0.5625 wide):

The same shift at both ends of a movement, or the demo would drift sideways while it plays. Scoring cannot see it: poseMatchScore centres both poses on their centroid first. That is a test, not an argument — checked for every shipped target at three offsets.

Defect B: the thresholds were read off a drawing

This is what the migration actually uncovered. rep_signals.dart records where the counter's thresholds came from: "Measured: … folded to the bottom -0.563", "bottom of the lunge -0.707", "lying flat 0.119". Those numbers reproduce exactly — but only by stretching the authored targets back by 1.5. The thresholds were read off the figures in pose_target.dart while those figures were 1.5× too wide, not off a person in front of a camera.

Three of the five signals are horizontal — a hinge is how far the shoulders travel forward of the hips — so the distortion went straight into them:

This is a live-behaviour defect, not a test artifact. The counter always compared these thresholds against real detector coordinates. So a user who stands exactly in the drawn hinge, lunge or situp silhouette does not reach the phase the counter is waiting for. Squat and pushup are unaffected — their signal reads the vertical axis only.

I did not fix it in this gate. Fixing it means measuring real repetitions on a phone, which is its own piece of work, and this gate was scoped to stay mechanical. Rather than skip the tests, they now assert the defect and name which end of each pair is unreachable. When the thresholds are recalibrated the tests will fail — which is the right way to find out.

The important part: every valid repetition had zero unscoreable frames. The four-joint minimum introduced when the arms stopped being judged is not biting in practice. And the later repetitions cluster tightly — 0.910, 0.913, 0.930, 0.936 — with no code change between them.

One observation that changes the next gate's plan. Depth alone does not separate counted from missed. Rep #2 was missed at 0.780 with a depth of 0.001; rep #4 counted at 0.840 with 0.000. What differed was the rest of the body — #2 carried shoulder 0.13 and ankle 0.20 normalised residuals against #4's 0.09 and 0.05. A cue built on depth alone would have said "squat lower" on a repetition whose depth was fine — exactly the failure that got the old depth rule withdrawn.

Review

Internal specialists first, GPT-PM second, in that order. The internal pass found two real gaps, both in the tests rather than the code:

  1. The invariance tests could not catch an unconverted target. Every "live" pose was built from the same target it was then compared against, so a target missed by the multiply would have gone on scoring 1.0 against itself on every frame shape. Closed: the pre-migration coordinates of all 13 mechanically converted targets — 78 joints — are pinned in a table read out of git by a script rather than retyped by hand.
  2. The defect-B guard asserted only "not both ends work". A completely different breakage satisfies that too. It now names which end is unreachable per movement and asserts both.

GPT-PM returned APPROVE with two MINOR notes, both about my prose:

  • "exactly nil" for rounded constants — corrected above;
  • I had described 0.6 as "the offset at which a joint stops counting". It is not: poseMatchScore averages the offsets of all scored joints and divides that mean by 0.6. There is no per-landmark rejection threshold at all. Left standing, that sentence would have sent the next gate hunting for a rule that does not exist.

Revised order

I proposed the diagnostic-cues gate next. GPT-PM reordered it, and I agree:

  1. POSE_TARGET_ISOTROPIC_COORDINATE_MIGRATION — closed.
  2. FORM_COACH_REP_SIGNAL_DEVICE_RECALIBRATION — next. Hinge, lunge, situp.
  3. FORM_COACH_SQUAT_DIAGNOSTIC_CUES — after that.

The reasoning is simple: cues improve the explanation of a path that already works, while defect B means three shipped movements ask for a pose their counter will not accept. Correctness before refinement.

The recalibration method is fixed in advance so it cannot drift. Do not simply substitute −0.717 / −0.832 / 0.177 for −0.600 / −0.720 / 0.160. That repeats the original methodological error inside the corrected coordinate system — reading a threshold off a drawing again. It needs distributions from real device repetitions, both correctly executed and deliberately incomplete; spread and noise first, then hysteresis bands with visible separation. The drawn silhouette is a sanity check, never the calibration dataset.

Where the two complaints stand

  • "Arm position decided the verdict" — closed in the previous gate (elbow and wrist are drawn but not judged). Today's run confirms it: eight counted repetitions, zero unscoreable frames.
  • "It says 'you did not reach the silhouette' instead of naming the actual fault" — not closed. That is the diagnostic-cues gate, now third rather than second. The material for it is already collected, and the first thing it says is that the cue cannot be built on depth.

What this gate does not prove

  • On the reference phone the migration is nil by construction, so a run there confirms nothing regressed rather than confirming a benefit. The multi-aspect gain only shows on hardware with a different camera ratio, which is not in hand.
  • The 9:16 bound in the test is an assumption about hardware, not something production enforces — the real ratio comes from sensor metadata. 9:16 is the narrowest common camera frame (phone screens are taller; sensors are not), so the bound stands, but it is an inference rather than a guarantee.
  • Two Home-screen golden tests fail at a 59.6% pixel diff. Not from this change, and provably so: lib/features/home/ contains no reference to form_check, and that baseline was last touched by an unrelated commit, f819820.
  • The first review run, from the codex checkout, could not reach the orchestrator and wrote a fail-open receipt — meaning the commit was permitted by an attempt at review, not by a review. The real round ran second, from the checkout that owns the live process.
