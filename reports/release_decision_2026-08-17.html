01The correction that made this report necessary

The previous reconciliation listed three reasons for NOT_RELEASE_READY, and the third was "nothing is pushed." That was a category error.

An unpushed branch says something about where the code is. It says nothing about whether the product is safe to ship.

Listing it beside F014 implied the two were the same kind of fact, and it made the verdict look better-supported than it was — three reasons instead of one. The claim is withdrawn. Delivery state now has its own dimension, and it is reported even when it is uninteresting, which is most of the time.

02Engineering status

F014 — the one open finding with direct safety impact

ENGINEERING_REMEDIATED, residual CLINICAL_POLICY_NOT_DEFINED.

The app now declines to build a personalised programme for a user who reports being pregnant or recently postpartum, and refers them. It encodes no trimester, no due date, no postpartum week and no risk score, and it removes not one exercise from the library — a test asserts it filters zero rows and sets no intensity ceiling, because it has no clinical basis on which to prefer one movement over another.

Enforcement is a single condition in wholePersonBlocks. That is the point rather than a shortcut: it is what allowsAnyTraining reads and what every prescribing surface already consults, so generation, enrolment, the planner, the AI coach, deep links and the in-workout picker were covered by one edit — and a new prescribing surface cannot miss it by forgetting a check, because there is no separate check to forget.

Mutation-proven: disabling that one condition fails 9 tests.

Not CLINICALLY_VALIDATED. A conservative refusal is not medical validation. Defining an actual perinatal exercise policy is clinical work under D1.

03Clinical authority status

D1 and H3 are unchanged, and are the entire verdict

D1 = EXTERNAL_CLINICAL_VALIDATION_REQUIRED · H3 = HOLD

Measured today: 1,887 exercise rows, 1,527 carrying at least one safety tag, 360 carrying none. The tags come from deterministic rules over movement names and primary muscles, with per-row provenance. That is a real screen and not a clinical one, and the product says so on screen — kSafetyTagsClinicallyReviewed = false drives a disclosure written to remove itself the day a clinician signs off.

What is new is that the review is now askable: core/review/CLINICAL_VALIDATION_HANDOFF.md states the current behaviour, asks seven bounded questions instead of "is SPTR safe?", and defines the structured result that must come back. A comment saying "looks good" is explicitly not validation evidence.

04Could CONDITIONAL_RELEASE be defensible?

Analysed rather than voted on, because the instruction was explicit that it must not be decided by vote.

What could ship untouched

Everything that does not depend on a safety tag: the exercise library as reference content, manual workout logging, the plate calculator, progress photos, body composition, subscriptions, account management, data export, the equipment scanner as an identification aid, and the AI coach in its already-gated form.

What could not

Everything whose output is a prescription: programme generation and enrolment, the adaptive planner, the For-You ranking, and exercise substitution. Each of those hands a user a specific movement, and the claim that unsuitable movements were removed rests entirely on tags no clinician has seen.

Why that subset is not a smaller version of this product

The honest reading of the split is that the disabled half is the product. SPTR's stated purpose is programmes built around what a person can actually do; a build with generation, planning and substitution removed is an exercise encyclopaedia with a logbook. That may be a legitimate thing to ship — but it is a different product, not a conditional release of this one, and calling it the latter would be the silent conversion the instruction warns against.

Does UNKNOWN genuinely fail closed?

Yes, and this was checked rather than assumed. An untagged row is withheld from an injured user rather than shown; an unfinished questionnaire refuses prescriptions while keeping browsing; the four unenforceable restrictions are stated to the user rather than silently filtering nothing. F013 was raised as a fail-open, contested by a blinded reader, re-read, and not upheld.

So the failure mode is not "unsafe exercise recommended". It is "a claim of screening that rests on unreviewed rules" — which is a claims problem, and is exactly what D1 exists to resolve.

Would a limited beta change the risk?

It changes the blast radius, not the claim. A beta user reading "exercises that conflict with your injury were removed" is relying on the same unreviewed tags as a general-release user. What a restricted release genuinely buys is the ability to gather the real-world evidence that several open questions need — including the held-out recognition set — under an explicit, prominent statement of what has and has not been reviewed.

Conclusion

No credible safe subset exists that is still this product. PRODUCT_RELEASE_VERDICT = NOT_RELEASE_READY stands, on clinical authority alone.

A restricted internal or clinical-pilot mode is describable and is described above. It is not being declared, because declaring it here would be exactly the quiet conversion of a full release into a beta that the instruction forbids. It needs its own decision, with its own disclosures.

The registry's first real finding

Two entries, both measured. equipment_v1 is bundled and champion — by default rather than by evaluation, since it is the only model that has ever shipped, and the registry says so rather than implying it won something. Its hash matches the training pipeline's own output, which is genuine provenance.

equipment_v2 is deliberately not marked challenger. A challenger is a state reached by passing a gate; no gate has run, the held-out set does not exist, and its one real-world number is below any product bar. Calling it a challenger because it is newer is precisely the drift the registry exists to stop.

Also corrected: the strategy document says v2 has 29 classes; its own labels.json lists 37. The registry carries the measured value and the prose is now fenced against it.

First CT candidate — scored, not assumed

Four candidates across eleven criteria in core/ml/CT_CANDIDATE_DECISION.md. Equipment recognition wins on 23/33; recommendation and search ranking score 13 each and are removed from contention by a single zero — there is no product telemetry, so there is no impression log to learn a ranking from.

The uncomfortable cell is the scanner's own zero: privacy risk. Retraining it continuously means gym photographs leaving devices, which is the most sensitive collection this product could add. So the winner is blocked, and not by a modelling problem.

Content QA prioritisation scores higher (31/33) and is named the first deliverable rather than the CT candidate, because it is not retraining at all — it is a ranked query over data already collected, with no model and no privacy consequence. Collapsing the two would have been the mistake.

Next executable ML milestone

Bring D:/tools/equipment-model under version control. It is not a git repository — verified — which is why training_code_commit is UNKNOWN for both registered models and why neither can be regenerated by anyone who does not hold that directory. It is the cheapest item in the entire ML programme, it blocks every reproducibility claim downstream, and it requires no decision from anyone.
