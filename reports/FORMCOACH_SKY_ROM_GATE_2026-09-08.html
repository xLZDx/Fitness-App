Why the ROM reference did not become clinical

The numbers (hip internal rotation 30–40°, external 40–60°, tibial rotation 10–15°) existed only as prose in the 8 August decision-log entry. Their provenance is a photograph of a poster and a blog article. A search confirmed the values match the benchmarks published in the usual clinical references, but "matches something widely repeated" is not a citation, and the authoritative goniometry references are copyrighted.

So the verdict is to narrow the claim: the artifact carries clinical_use: false, every row carries the origin it actually has, and nothing here may be shown to a user as a medical finding. A side benefit of that choice is that the artifact then depends on no third-party licence at all, because no external table is reproduced.

And why it is not wired to rep counting

This correction came from GPT-PM's review and was verified against source. The rep counter drives every movement from a three-landmark sagittal angle — squat is leftHip/leftKnee/leftAnkle. Every row in the reference is a transverse-plane rotation. They are different measurements about different axes, and the fact that both are expressed in degrees is not a relationship between them. Constraining a 70–172° knee measurement by a 30–60° hip-rotation interval would have produced validation that reads as rigorous and means nothing.

A note on evidence hygiene

docker pull reported exited with code 0 while in fact doing nothing: the daemon was not running, and the | tail in the pipeline replaced the real exit status. It was caught by reading the output rather than trusting the code. Recorded in the decision log because it is this project's recurring defect in miniature — a green result that proves nothing.
