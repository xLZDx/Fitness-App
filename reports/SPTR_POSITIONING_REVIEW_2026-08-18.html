The finding that changed the verdict

The concept rests on two numbers: the nameplate identifies 18 of 18, the classifier 5 of 18. Read at source, they do not mean what a landing page would make them mean.

Of the operator's 30 gym photographs, 18 could be labelled at all — precisely because the machine's name is printed on it. The remaining 12 are wide room shots and are ungradeable. So "18/18" is the human labeller reading a nameplate to establish ground truth; it is not a measurement of the app's OCR pipeline succeeding on those frames. And "5/18" belongs to the 29-class v2 model, which the app does not load.

Meanwhile the recognition path that actually runs in production is cloud Gemini, and the measurement plan states plainly that it was never measured. The hero mechanic currently has no accuracy figure that a page could honestly print.

04

Brand verdict — keep SPTR, drop "Digital Spotter"

The panel split here and then converged on evidence. The brand reviewer initially defended "digital" as the qualifier that limits the literal read; the competitive and safety reviewers argued for removing the gloss entirely. Three findings settled it.

  1. The word is crowded, on the exact store SPTR must ship into. "Spotter — Your Gym Reference" is live on Google Play and its description is this product's own pitch: an exercise library plus an AI that recognizes any gym machine from a photo, 1,300+ exercises. Beside it: Spotter: AI Workout Companion, SpotterAI, Spotter: Find fitness buddies, Spotter Fitness Trainer, plus several on iOS.
  2. The metaphor does not survive Russian. In Russian, споттер is predominantly an auto-body spot-welding tool. Lifters use the verb — подстрахуешь? — not a noun-title, and страховка primarily means an insurance policy. With RU shipping at full parity, a metaphor that has to be explained in half the product is a metaphor that has failed.
  3. It names the one function the product cannot perform. A spotter's job is to catch a failing lift. The exposure is not that anyone expects the phone to do that; it is that "your spotter is right there with you" grants permission to attempt a rep the user would not attempt alone. Combined with a declared 16+ audience — the users most likely to train alone and accept implied supervision — this is the wrong risk to take for a metaphor.

SPTR itself stays. It is already the applicationId suffix, which the build file itself notes can never change after the first Play upload, and no app was found under that string in either store. But it must never appear alone in the early phase: SPTR is a signature, not an introduction. Pair it permanently with a functional descriptor rather than a role claim.

Unresolved — needs counsel, not another search

The one US registration that would have blocked this — SPOTTER, Reg. 5285534, Class 009, covering fitness app software — was cancelled in March 2024. Two later filings and the Spotter Inc. portfolio could not be verified. Do not treat the name as cleared on the strength of this review.

08

Page architecture

The proposed seven sections are storytelling order. This is a recruitment page whose single job is producing named Android testers, so the order is different — and one section is cut entirely.

  1. Hero fused with the recognition demo. The mechanic is the value proposition; giving it its own scroll target wastes the only screen everyone sees. Above the fold: headline, a real screen capture of a scan, the primary CTA, and the "not on Play" line.
  2. Catalog and discovery. Scale proof, and the answer to "why not YouTube".
  3. Filtering around what you tell it. The actual differentiator among shipping features.
  4. What it won't tell you. The refusal behaviour — placed here deliberately, after every positive claim and immediately before the final ask.
  5. Final CTA with the install mechanics spelled out in full.

Cut for v1: the combined progress/recovery/privacy section. It bundles three claims nobody is on this page to evaluate. Keep exactly one privacy sentence beside the email field, where it does conversion work.

Why the honesty section goes at position 4, not the hero

A small disclosed negative increases choice likelihood, but only when it follows the positive information and is processed with low effort. Placement is therefore not a style question: the same content early produces the opposite effect. It also means one number per claim, never a methodology table — a table invites arithmetic ("so it's wrong one rep in eight"), which is exactly the high-effort processing that destroys the effect.

09

The "don't guess" strategy, judged on its merits

This is the strongest idea in the concept and the panel wants it kept. It is also the part most likely to be executed into a liability, so the distinctions matter.

Is it supported by code? Partly, and the parts differ. The rep-counter refusal is real, gated, tested and visible on screen. The scanner's uncertainty behaviour is also real — offline answers are never presented as settled, and the UI has a "Not sure — closest matches" state. But the absolute form of the slogan is falsified by three things at once: 360 exercises pass the injury filter untagged, the production recognition path has no measured accuracy, and the form checker is experimental by the team's own statement.

So sell the behaviour, not the absolute. "A spotter doesn't guess" is a claim about identity and can be disproved by one screenshot. "It shows no number rather than a wrong one" is a claim about a specific, demonstrable mechanism and is true.

On the percentages: keep them off the marketing surface. The denominator is invisible in a marketing context, benchmark conditions are not a gym floor at a bad angle, and a printed 1.00 is a claim of infallibility that the first miscounted set disproves. If numbers are published at all, they belong on a methodology page, as a band, always labelled counting accuracy — because the unqualified word is what lets a reader hear "correct form guaranteed".

The guardrail that makes this territory defensible

This positioning is only defensible while it is literally true. The moment the threshold is rounded, or the app quietly starts counting push-ups, the claim inverts into the most damaging one available. If the operator builds on this, the 0.80 threshold stops being a tuning constant and becomes a written product constraint.

10

Visual and motion direction

The stated direction — bright, light, premium, product-first, for adults 20–45 — fits the product and the audience, and the exclusions are right. Five principles carry it further than adjectives do.

  1. The product is the art direction. Real screens at real size, not floating devices in gradient space. This product's credibility comes from specificity; abstraction spends it.
  2. Show the gym as a place of confusion, not conquest. The reader's moment is standing in front of an unfamiliar machine — not a finishing rep. This also happens to avoid every image the safety review prohibits.
  3. Uncertainty needs a visual language. "Not sure — closest matches" is a real state and deserves design attention equal to the confident one. Designing the uncertain state well is the whole brand in one component.
  4. Bilingual by construction. Russian runs longer than English; a layout tuned to English line lengths breaks. Design in the longer language.
  5. Numbers are typography. Tabular figures, one number per claim, never a wall of statistics.

Real media plan

Everything the page needs already exists or can be captured in one afternoon in a real gym with the current build.

  • The scan, uncut, 12–20 s. Phone raised to a machine, the nameplate read, the result — including the confidence state. One take, no cuts, because the cut is where a viewer assumes the failure was hidden.
  • The refusal, 8 s. A push-up set where the count badge stays off and the coaching keeps running. This is the single most persuasive asset the product can produce.
  • The filter, 10 s. A knee flagged in the intake, then the same list before and after.
  • Three still screens for the catalog: male and female demonstration of the same exercise, and the Russian interface.
  • One honest failure, optional but recommended. A scan that returns "not sure" with alternatives. A brand built on declared uncertainty that only ever shows successes is making the claim it says it doesn't make.

Safety and privacy review

The single structural point, which outranks every wording note: under EU device rules, intended purpose is established by promotional material. The code does not decide whether this is a medical device — the landing page does. Copy like "trains around your injuries" can convert a wellness app into an unregistered medical device placed on the EU market, by a developer with no legal entity and no authorised representative.

Prohibited outright

Never say keeps you safe · prevents injury · trains around your injuries · respects your limits · knows your body · cleared / approved for you · contraindication (public-facing) · assessment · screening · rehab · physiotherapy · doesn't guess · guaranteed · 100 % · bare "accuracy" without "counting".

Never show barbell bench press or squat rack under load · anyone training to failure · a lone lifter in an empty gym · a phone placed where a human spotter would stand · green checkmarks or shield glyphs on an exercise card · medical iconography · anyone who reads as under 16 · any screenshot with a numeric form score visible.

Required, verbatim

Hero region, at body-copy weight: SPTR is not a spotter and is not a substitute for one. Never train to failure with free weights alone.

Footer and store listing: SPTR is a general fitness information app. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. Filtering hides exercises tagged for body areas you select; it is not a medical clearance and does not identify every exercise that may be a problem for you. Consult a healthcare professional before starting or changing an exercise programme — especially if you have an injury or medical condition, are pregnant, have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or have had recent surgery. SPTR is for users aged 16 and over.

Google Play's health declaration and the marketing copy are reviewed together, and inconsistency between them is itself the violation — near-term, store suspension is a far more likely consequence than any regulatory action.

The page would contradict the app

This is the sharpest finding in the safety review, and it is specific to this product: the app's own disclaimers are more honest than the proposed marketing. Every headline claim has a near-verbatim inversion already shipping in the interface.

Proposed lineWhat the app already tells the user
"A spotter doesn't guess. Neither does SPTR.""Rep counts and technique cues are approximate."
"Keeps you safe" / "avoids exercises that could hurt you""They do not judge whether a movement is safe for you."
"Trains around your injuries" / "respects your limits""No physiotherapist has reviewed the list yet."
"Your digital spotter""They are not coaching."
"Injury-aware" as a badge"Not a medical assessment… not a diagnosis."

The governing rule that falls out of this: the website may never claim more than the app's own disclaimers concede. The in-app language is not a constraint to work around — it is better copy than anything proposed, and it should be reused verbatim.

Why this matters more here than it would elsewhere

There is no company. Per the app's own terms, this is one independent developer in Chisinau with no legal entity — so there is no corporate veil, and under EU device rules a natural person can be the manufacturer. Marketing that creates a medical intended purpose would make an individual an unregistered device manufacturer placing a device on the EU market from outside it, without the authorised representative that non-EU manufacturers must appoint. He is also personally the data controller for the intake's health answers, and the revised EU product-liability regime now covers software explicitly.

The conclusion the panel draws is not "add more disclaimers". It is claim less. Every safety claim removed is exposure a single person does not personally carry — and since the removed claims were also the falsifiable ones, nothing of value is lost.

Privacy — the strongest verified material in the product

Progress photos: AES-256-GCM, a per-account key generated from a secure RNG and held in the hardware-backed keystore, explicitly excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, with no upload path in existence and no share or export flow — one was designed and deliberately not built because it would put a decrypted image into the system share sheet. Deleting the account removes both the ciphertext directory and the key. Pose analysis runs on device.

Two caveats that must travel with the claim: the photo index — dates, weights, notes — is stored in plaintext beside the encrypted images, and this is protection at rest on the device, not end-to-end encryption. The code says so itself, which is why the page may say "encrypted on your phone" and may not say "end-to-end".

Reviewer disagreements

Resolved — the spotter gloss. Brand argued "digital" limits the literal read and the metaphor's trust semantics are worth keeping; competitive and safety argued for removal. Resolved against keeping it, on three independent pieces of evidence (store crowding, Russian failure, 16+ audience). Brand's own research independently found the Play collision, which narrowed the gap.

Resolved — the accuracy numbers. Growth wanted one figure per movement on the page; safety wanted none. Settled by a fact neither had: the recognition figures do not measure the shipped configuration at all. Rep-counter numbers are real and may appear on a methodology page; recognition numbers may not be published in any form until the production path is measured.

Standing, unresolved, for the operator. Whether to publish the 0.80 threshold as a binding public commitment. The panel recommends it and cannot decide it — it converts a tuning constant into a promise, and that is a business decision about future freedom, not an evidence question.

Open verification gap. Whether the workouts feature consumes already-filtered data or bypasses the injury filter could not be settled in this pass — no direct call site exists in that feature. Until it is settled, no page may claim filtering is universal across the app.

19

The website this should be, and the decision

A single-screen-deep recruitment page, in Russian first, whose job is to turn a stranger into a named Android tester the operator can reach. It opens on the moment of standing in front of an unfamiliar machine and shows a real, uncut capture of the app reading that machine's printed name. It proves scale with the catalog, proves care with the filter, and closes — immediately before the ask — with the one thing no competitor will copy: a screen where the app declines to show a number it cannot stand behind. It states plainly that it is an Android beta, not on Google Play, run by one person in Chisinau, and it says in the hero that it is not a spotter and does not replace one.

It publishes no accuracy percentage it has not measured, no store badge it does not have, and no safety claim at all.

Implementation decision

Ready for website design and implementation: NO. Six blockers stand, and four of them are product-side rather than copy-side — the name decision, the launcher label, the signing key, and the unmeasured recognition path. None takes long. All must precede design, because each one changes what the page is allowed to say.

The recommendation is not to wait for Google Play. A new personal developer account cannot reach production without first running a closed test with twelve testers for fourteen consecutive days — so the tester list is not an alternative to Play, it is the prerequisite. That is the strongest argument for building this page now, at five sections, as a recruitment tool rather than a product launch.
