01

Conceded, and the narrow part that survives

"A refusal cannot be copied without deleting a competitor's own demo" is withdrawn. The counter is correct and simple: a competitor keeps automatic counting for supported movements and softens it below confidence; keeps recognition and exposes candidates when unsure; keeps the AI answer and adds the source. Nothing is deleted, and the engineering cost is small. That sentence claimed a technical monopoly where there is only brand discipline.

One narrower version does survive, and it is worth keeping separate from the general claim it was inflated into: for a product whose entire storefront is a push-up counter — and the rep-counter category's storefront is push-ups — adopting this rule means shipping a blank space where the demo is. That is a real disincentive, but it applies to a handful of single-feature apps, not to the category.

EVIDENCE · RESTRAINT · RECOVERY is adopted and replaces "three refusals". It is more accurate — only the middle one is a refusal — and it describes what the user gets rather than what the product withholds. As an internal doctrine: show the evidence, don't fake certainty, learn from the miss.

Also accepted: auditable identification is a trust mechanic, not the brand. A brand that means "shows you the text it read" is narrower in two years than the product will be.

03

The pilot, and what it found in one pass

Rather than hand over an untested questionnaire, the instrument was run: three respondents, one concept each, monadic, no context about the project, no marketing vocabulary permitted, five-second exposure framing. This is a smoke test of the instrument with a single synthetic respondent per cell. It is not evidence about the market and no decision should rest on the numbers alone. What it can do — and did — is show which questions produce signal and which produce nothing.

Finding 1 — the hook is the part the user needs least

«Реально важно — "сколько я жал в прошлый раз" и настройки сиденья/упора, вот это бесит каждый раз заново. "Что это за тренажёр" — почти не нужно, воспользуюсь раза два из любопытства и всё.» Cell C, asked bluntly which of the three promises matters

Six rounds argued about whether recognition is a moat. The respondent skipped that question and answered a better one: for a semi-experienced user it is barely a benefit. Identification acquires; setup and memory retain. That is exactly the order the product backlog already has — and exactly the opposite of the order every version of the hero has used.

Finding 2 — the evidence concept produced a trust paradox

"More trust in the people, slightly less confidence in the thing working… if you have to say three times that you'll admit mistakes, maybe there are a lot of them." Cell B, on whether transparency increased trust

This is the nutrition-category caution arriving in a live answer: restraint stacked three deep stops reading as integrity and starts reading as a warning label. It does not kill the mechanic — the same respondent volunteered "it admits when it doesn't know" as the thing they would tell a friend — but it caps the dose. One restraint line converts; three compound into doubt.

Finding 3 — the strongest analytical concept was the least memorable

Cell B scored highest in round six's rubric at 33 points and lowest here on next-day memorability, with no unaided name recall at all: "I'd remember 'the app that shows what it read' long before I'd remember what it's called." A concept can be simultaneously the best argument and the worst brand vehicle. Round seven's instinct to demote it from positioning to proof is supported.

Two smaller things worth keeping

  • The "no stickers, no gym setup" line backfired mildly in cell A — it introduced a worry that did not previously exist: «я и не думал, что что-то надо ставить, и на секунду засомневался». It is a real competitive advantage against half the field and it should stay in the body copy, but not in the first three lines where it answers an unasked question.
  • Cell C left its own data source unexplained — «Не понял, откуда оно знает МОИ настройки». The concept silently mixes authored guidance (the seat position for this machine type) with personal history (what you lifted). Those are different promises and the page must not blur them.
04

A structural bound the respondent felt before the analysis found it

«…но я не уверен, что оно реально узнает те железки, что стоят у нас… старые и китайские тренажёры без опознавательных знаков.» Cell C, unprompted
The nameplate mechanic cannot address most of the gym

Measured in the registry: of 69 equipment types, 28 are selectorised strength machines — the only ones that plausibly carry a printed exercise name. Twelve are free weights and bodyweight, which can never carry one. By exercise count, machines that could bear a nameplate reach 533 of the 1,384 mapped exercises; free weights and bodyweight alone account for 540.

So the reading-the-label story addresses roughly two fifths of the mapped catalog and none of the barbell half of the gym. That does not invalidate it — the machines it covers are precisely the ones people cannot identify — but it does mean the mechanic cannot be the whole product story, and the field study must measure nameplate prevalence within the strength subset rather than across gyms in general.

05

What the pilot could not catch

All three cells passed the safety question. That question asks directly whether the app appears to supervise or protect you, and a direct question cannot catch a risk that lives in the combination of two lines neither of which is a safety claim on its own. A separate adversarial pass was run against the stimuli for exactly that class of defect. Three of its findings change the stimuli before any real panel sees them.

A "without asking anyone" is the wrong kind of alone

Two problems in one phrase. It makes staff and trainers into things to be avoided, which turns a competence product into a shame product and invites the flat retort the trainer is free, just ask him. And it interacts with the name: a product called after a spotter, promising you need not ask anyone, is one visual of a lone user under load away from reading as this replaces the person who would otherwise be standing there. In Russian the register is harsher still — «чтобы никто не узнал» is concealment, not discretion.

The emotional promise survives; the solitude promise does not. Rewrite to the outcome — so you can work it out yourself — and keep every frame of footage away from a loaded barbell over a solo lifter.

C "Set it" is the worst available ambiguity in this category

In a fitness context the first reading of set is a set of reps, not a machine adjustment. The middle verb therefore reads as set-logging, which points the concept head-on at Hevy and Strong on a feature the product does not have and is not planning. This is not a wording preference — it means cell C as written tests a different product than the one intended, and the pilot respondent's confusion about where the settings come from is the mild version of the same defect.

B the restraint line inverts at glance speed

"It never shows certainty it doesn't have" is a double negative. Read in five seconds it collapses to it's never wrong — the precise opposite of the intent, and the one misreading that would make the restraint doctrine actively dishonest. There is no clean Russian form either. The behaviour is right; the sentence has to go.

Where the adversarial pass overreaches

  • "A is LTV-hostile — it buys a cohort that stops being that cohort in six weeks." Directionally worth testing, but the pilot's respondent was a 32-year-old with real gym experience and read A exactly as intended without feeling addressed as a novice. Keep A; add a screener so the beginner/experienced split can be read separately rather than assumed.
  • "B misreads as a document scanner." True of the headline alone, not of the stimulus — the frame contains a gym and a machine shroud. The pilot respondent comprehended it correctly. A headline-only critique overstates a risk the visual already answers.
  • "Drop C before testing." The conclusion is right and it was already round seven's ruling, but note what would have been lost: the single most valuable line in this entire round came out of cell C's respondent. Drop the concept, keep the question that produced the finding.

What would make the whole test worthless

  • Unequal production values between cells. If one concept gets better footage, the test measures the footage. Same gym, same machine, same device, same day.
  • Testing copy the product cannot deliver. Two of cell C's three verbs are unbuilt; if the test wins on those and the build never follows, the result is a plan for a page that must be retracted.
  • Synthetic respondents treated as market evidence. This pilot is a check on the instrument. Numbers from one simulated person per cell cannot rank concepts; they can only show which questions discriminate.
  • Language drift, and worse, literal translation. One pilot respondent answered in Russian, one in English, from the same persona brief — fix the response language per cell or the verbatims are not comparable. The deeper trap: A and B live almost entirely in register, so a literally translated stimulus is a different concept, and pooling the two languages compares two products. Localise as concept, then back-translate to check what was actually promised.
One structural addition

Monadic exposure is correct for comprehension, message playback and unaided recall — comparison contaminates all three, which is why the pilot ran that way. It is weak for the ship decision: monadic top-box scores across concepts tend to collapse into a narrow band that discriminates nothing, and a warm concept can out-like a useful one while converting worse. Add one small forced-rank cell that sees all three and analyse it separately. Do not let that cell also answer the comprehension questions.

08

Decision and next gate

  • Final positioning: still not selected, and the pilot supports waiting rather than settles it.
  • Cell A carries the hero on current evidence, with the camera mechanic as its second line rather than its first.
  • Cell B becomes one line in the body — its own respondent proved the mechanic transmits and the stacking does not.
  • Cell C is dropped from the test, not merely demoted — it describes an unbuilt product, carries the only implied-safety problem in the set, and its central verb is ambiguous in the worst possible way for this category. A test whose winning answer could not be shipped is spend with no outcome. Move its sample into an A/B split that runs the evidence line as a variable rather than a concept.

Four things now block a real test, and none of them is another review: the three stimulus amendments in §05, which must land before any panel sees the cells; the nameplate field study, bounded to strength machines; the two backlog items the respondent named as the ones that matter; and a matched production shoot so the cells differ in message and nothing else.

The formula after eight rounds

SPTR — short for Spotter. Helps you understand what is in front of you, remembers the context that matters, shows what its answer is based on, and does not pretend when it has no answer yet.

The pilot's contribution to that sentence is the order: what the product remembers is worth more to a real user than what it recognises, and the page has been leading with the wrong half.
