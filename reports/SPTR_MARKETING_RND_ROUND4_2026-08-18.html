The strongest position this product can own is already built, and nobody noticed it for three rounds

Under the new rule — a promise the current build cannot keep becomes a product requirement, not a deletion — the search widened. It found something narrower and better than any of the previous candidates: every gym machine worth naming has its name printed on it in English, most of this product's market cannot read English, and the app already reads that English label and answers in Russian. That capability ships today, no competitor in this category has it, and it converts the one mechanic everyone called a commodity into the one thing that is hard to follow.

A

Ten differentiated territories, ranked

Ranked by differentiation × conversion value ÷ (build cost + proof cost). Status uses the operator's four classes.

01Exists now

The machine is labelled in English. The user is not.

"Сфотографируй тренажёр — он объяснит по-русски."

Pain
Nautilus, Precor, Life Fitness, Technogym and Star Trac print the exercise name on the shroud — in English, everywhere in the world. A Russian-speaking beginner in Chisinau, Almaty or Kazan is standing in front of an instruction they cannot read, on a machine they cannot name, and the internet's answer is also in English.
Gap
All four camera-scan competitors are English-only products. A check for a Russian-language machine-recognition app surfaced none — Russian gym apps are planners, trackers and AI-coach chat. The mechanic is a commodity in English and appears unoccupied in Russian.
Status
Built and verified. All 69 equipment types carry English and Russian aliases in one list, so an English nameplate resolves regardless of interface language; the catalog is Russian for 1,887 of 1,887 entries with full step lists; UI strings are at 1,101 of 1,101 parity.
Build
None for the capability. What is missing is the claim: nobody has measured the anchor end to end on non-Latin-market photos, and that measurement is the only thing standing between this and a headline.
Proof
Run the text anchor over a labelled set of real nameplate photos and report the hit rate. This is the single highest-value measurement in the whole backlog.
Value
Acquisition, brand memory, and a genuine moat — a competitor has to build a full Russian catalog, not translate a landing page.
Verdict
Bet the positioning on this.
02Committed before marketing

The machine remembers you

"You've been here before. 45 kg, ten reps, two weeks ago."

Pain
Recognition is a one-time novelty — both earlier rounds flagged it and neither solved it. Nobody scans the same machine twice to be told what it is. They would scan it every week to be told what they lifted on it.
Gap
Every logger is exercise-anchored: "leg press — 45 kg" lives in a list you have to go and open. Nothing surfaces your own history at the moment you are physically standing at the machine, because nothing else knows which machine you are standing at.
Status
Mostly assembled already. Log rows carry exerciseId, catalog exercises carry equipmentId for 1,384 of 1,887, so machine-level history is derivable from shipped data with no schema change. And suggestNextWeight() already exists with real rules — deload 5 % after a "too hard", +5 % after three easy sessions at target reps.
Build
Low Group existing history by equipment id and render it on the scan result. The physical-instance version — this leg press rather than leg presses in general — needs a new gym-and-unit entity and is High; do not promise it yet.
Proof
Trivially demonstrable on video. No measurement needed.
Value
Retention. This is what turns the hook into a habit, and it is the cheapest high-value item in the backlog.
03Committed before marketing

Setup, not identity — the seat and the pin

"Knowing the name is the easy part. Where does the seat go?"

Pain
Nobody is defeated by not knowing a machine's name. They are defeated by the seat being at someone else's height, the pad hitting the wrong part of the shin, and the pin being in a hole they cannot see the logic of. Naming the machine and then leaving the user to guess the geometry solves the smaller half of the problem.
Gap
The whole category treats an exercise as the atom. Setup belongs to the machine, not the exercise, which is why an exercise library cannot carry it — and why an app that identifies machines is the only one shaped to hold it.
Status
Absent, and the content is not hiding in the catalog. With word-boundary matching, only 467 of 1,887 exercises mention seat, pad, pin, adjust or height at all, and just 83 of the 186 machine-labelled ones. Neither the exercise nor the equipment record has a field for it.
Build
Low–Medium — and this is the finding that matters: because setup attaches to the machine, the authoring job is 69 records, not 1,887. One nullable field plus 69 short setup cards, in two languages.
Proof
Content review, not measurement. Keep it mechanical — where things go, not what is safe.
Value
Acquisition and trust. It is also the most demonstrable thing on a landing page: a photo of a seat at the wrong height needs no explanation.
04Exists now

The machine it doesn't know gets added because you pointed at it

"Not in here yet. Now it is on the list, with your photo."

Pain
Every recognition product fails silently on the machine it has never seen and returns the nearest wrong answer.
Gap
Unoccupied, and structurally awkward for anyone whose pitch is that their AI recognises everything. Admitting a miss contradicts their headline; here it is the headline.
Status
Built, persisted and user-visible. An unrecognised machine produces a record carrying what the model could tell plus the user's photograph as the only evidence of what they actually pointed at, shown as "Content being prepared" and moving through a real lifecycle to inCatalog or declined. Cards from two gyms merge into one, so a machine two people photograph is prioritised twice.
Build
None for the mechanism. Marketable only once there is a stated fill rate — a promise like "photographed machines get added inside a month" needs someone to actually do it.
Proof
Publish the fill rate honestly, or make no time promise at all.
Value
Trust proof plus a compounding data asset: coverage of the machines actually present in the gyms of one specific market. That is the only moat here that grows while you sleep.
05Committed before marketing

It tells you when it doesn't know — everywhere, not in places

"Show what you know. Show when you don't."

Pain
Confident wrong answers, which this category produces constantly and never labels.
Gap
Unoccupied at the hard end. One nutrition app widens a number into a range; nobody withholds a number entirely.
Status
Real but inconsistent. Three genuine behaviours ship — the scanner's "Not sure — closest matches", offline answers never presented as settled, and a rep count withheld below a 0.80 measured bar — but there is no shared notion of uncertainty in the codebase. Confidence is an ad-hoc double? in at least three places with three different shapes.
Build
Medium One uncertainty type and one way of showing it, applied across scanner, form check and any generative answer. This is the clearest case of marketing defining an engineering constraint: the territory is only ownable if the behaviour is structural rather than three coincidences.
Proof
An auditable rule per surface, and a written threshold that cannot be quietly rounded.
Value
Trust and brand memory. Not the acquisition hook — nobody downloads an app to be told it is unsure.
06Concept hypothesis

The machine is taken and you still have forty minutes

"Occupied. Here are two machines that are free and do the same job."

Pain
The most universal gym frustration there is, and it happens at 18:00 to everyone who works.
Gap
Nothing addresses it in the moment. Planners assume the plan survives contact with a full gym.
Status
Absent as a feature; the data supports it — exercises carry muscles, primaryMuscles and equipmentId, which is what a same-pattern-different-equipment query needs.
Build
Medium Substitution logic plus a way to know what is free — which is the hard half, and the honest version is asking the user rather than pretending to sense it.
Proof
Substitution quality needs review by someone who trains; a bad swap is worse than no swap.
Value
Strong acquisition hook, high emotional recognition, medium defensibility — a competitor can add a substitution list without knowing the machine.
07Concept hypothesis

Your gym builds its own profile while you train

"Three weeks in, it knows your gym better than you do."

Pain
Recommendations for equipment your gym does not have.
Gap
Half-occupied, and the half that is missing is the good half. Fitbod has multi-gym profiles with a user-ticked equipment list and per-increment weights — maintained by hand. Nobody builds the inventory automatically from what the user has photographed, and nobody holds it at machine-model resolution.
Status
Absent. There is no gym entity anywhere in the model; the recognition history is keyed by machine type and deliberately merges across gyms.
Build
High New entity, new relations, and it is the prerequisite for the physical-instance version of territory 02.
Value
Retention and a second compounding data asset. Correct as a year-two bet, wrong as a launch promise.
08Exists now

You choose what to leave out

"Tell it what to skip. It stops offering it."

Pain
Being shown, session after session, the exercise you already know you cannot do.
Gap
Coarse versions exist in Fitbod, JEFIT and Alpha Progression. The resolution here is higher — 1,527 of 1,887 exercises tagged across nine body areas — but resolution is reproducible with budget.
Status
Built and live on the catalog list, equipment detail, the planner and deep links.
Build
None. What changes is the framing: this is user control, not safety. Reframed that way it needs no health claim and carries no regulatory weight, which is what previous rounds spent their energy on.
Value
Supporting differentiation. Real, but it does not carry a page on its own.
09Exists now

Count only what can be counted

"No reliable count, no number."

Status
Built: thresholds measured against 6,160 labelled repetitions, a 0.80 bar, and a visible badge where the number is withheld.
Gap
Unoccupied and economically awkward to copy — the rep-counter category's storefront is push-ups, the exact movement withheld here.
Value
The best proof asset in the product, and the right closing argument on the page. Not the opening one.
Note
Publish the behaviour, not the percentages. A printed 1.00 is disproved by the first miscount.
10Exists now

The photographs of your own body never leave the phone

"Encrypted on your phone. Never uploaded, never backed up, never exported."

Status
Verified in depth: AES-256-GCM, per-account key in the hardware keystore, excluded from cloud backup and device transfer, no upload path in the codebase, no export flow — one was designed and deliberately not built — and deletion removes both ciphertext and key. Pose analysis is on-device too.
Scope carefully
The machine scanner is not on-device: photos go to Google Gemini and are stored in the EU, which the shipped privacy policy already discloses. Claim the photos and the pose, never the scan.
Value
Trust, for a product that asks people to photograph their own body. A supporting block, not a territory.
The pain behind territories 01, 03 and hook 10 is measured, not assumed

Published survey work on gym intimidation lands repeatedly on equipment as the specific trigger rather than on bodies or fitness level: around 71 % of gym newcomers report worrying about not knowing how to use the equipment properly; in one segmentation 41 % of gym anxiety traces to not knowing how to use machines; roughly a third of 3,000 surveyed UK members felt intimidated using equipment for the first time; and "afraid to ask for help" recurs among the top stated reasons. Two machines are named most often as the ones people cannot work out — the Smith machine at nearly 40 % and the assisted pull-up at 37.6 %. Both are in this catalog.

That is the strongest external validation in four rounds, and it validates the machine-shaped reading of the problem rather than the coaching-shaped one the category sells. It also says the audience is wider than beginners: over 40 % report carrying the feeling for more than five years.

Never claim — removed permanently, not deferred

Injury prevention, safety outcomes, medical clearance, "trains around your injuries", anything implying supervision during a lift, and any percentage presented as real-world accuracy. These fail not because they are unbuilt but because they would still be wrong after being built. Auto-deload also leaves the marketing set — Fitbod's recovery model occupies it — and Wear OS and the coach marketplace stay off until they are real.

B

Three brand directions

Direction 1 — SPTR = SPOTTER, steelmanned

The strongest version drops the catching and keeps the judgement. A spotter's actual work, watched honestly in any gym, is three things: knowing this equipment, telling you the truth about the set, and saying "not this one, not today". Two of those three transfer perfectly to software, and the third is the one that creates the liability — so the ownable version is the one who tells you the truth, not the one who is there when it fails.

Where it still loses: six live products already use the word, one of them on Google Play with this product's own pitch, and in Russian the word means a body-shop welding tool. A brand that has to be explained in half its market is a brand that spends its impressions on spelling.

Verdict: keep as an English-only mnemonic under the mark. Not the promise.

Direction 2 — the reader of machines Recommended

Derived from the only thing this product uniquely knows: it reads the identity of the specific physical object in front of the user. Not a coach, not a planner, not a companion — a reader. It reads what the manufacturer printed and hands it back in the user's own language, with the exercises attached.

Lockup: SPTR — reads the machine. In Russian: SPTR — читает тренажёр. The Russian version is the stronger of the two, which has not been true of any other candidate in four rounds.

What must be true at launch: the anchor's hit rate on real nameplates must be measured, and the Russian answer must be as complete as the English one — which it already is.

What a competitor must do to take it: build a Russian catalog of 1,887 exercises with video, then accumulate coverage of the machines actually standing in the gyms of a market they do not operate in. That is not a sprint.

Direction 3 — the anti-category brand

A tool defined by its restraint: it shows what it knows, shows when it doesn't, and adds what it lacks because you pointed at it. Genuinely unoccupied and philosophically consistent with three shipping behaviours.

Why it is not first: restraint is a reason to trust, not a reason to install. As the primary promise it asks a stranger to admire a negative. It is the second paragraph of the page, and it is what makes the first paragraph believable.

Recommendation

Direction 2 as the promise, Direction 3 as the trust layer, Direction 1 retained as an English-only name story. The operator's objective — that SPTR be remembered as Spotter — survives in English and is replaced in Russian by something better: a name that is remembered for what it does to the machine in front of you.

Twelve hooks, distinct by angle

Each is a different situation, not a rephrasing. Collision-checked against the live competitor lines established in earlier rounds.

The machine is labelled in English. You don't have to be.
Тренажёр подписан по-английски. Тебе — не обязательно.
Angle: language barrier · needs the anchor measured · no competitor operates in Russian · top pick: it is the only hook that is simultaneously true today, unoccupied, and stronger in Russian than in English.
You've used this machine before. It remembers what you lifted.
Ты уже был на этом тренажёре. Он помнит, сколько ты поднял.
Angle: return visit · needs machine-level history grouping (Low) · loggers are exercise-anchored, so nobody says this · top pick for retention.
Knowing what it's called is the easy part.
Понять, как называется — это простое. Сложное — куда ставить сиденье.
Angle: setup over identity · needs 69 setup cards · directly attacks the four scan competitors' entire proposition · top pick for differentiation against the commodity.
That machine in the corner you've never touched.
Angle: the avoided machine. Both prior rounds converged on this insight independently, which is the strongest signal any of them produced. Warm, specific, honest today.
The rack is taken. You've still got forty minutes.
Angle: the occupied machine. Highest emotional recognition of any line here; needs substitution logic and an honest way to know what is free.
New gym. Same forty minutes.
Angle: unfamiliar gym, travel, moving city. Works with today's capability; weaker in a market where people train at one gym for years.
When it isn't sure, it says so.
Если не уверен — так и скажет.
Angle: visible uncertainty. True in three places today, structural after the shared-uncertainty work. Trust layer, not a hero.
It reads the label. It doesn't guess from the shape.
Angle: the mechanism itself. Explains why it works rather than that it works — and it is the sentence that separates this from a photo classifier.
Doesn't know your machine? Photograph it. Then it will.
Angle: the coverage flywheel. Unoccupied, and it turns the product's gap into the reason to participate. Needs a fill-rate someone will honour.
Nobody has to know it's your first week.
Angle: the social cost of looking lost. Emotionally the strongest territory in the whole category and, as far as this review found, unclaimed by any app's marketing. Handle without condescension.
Ten exercises on the machine you thought did one.
Angle: hidden capacity. Honest for the well-covered machines only — the median machine in the registry has four exercises and 29 of 69 have three or fewer, so this hook is a content commitment before it is copy.
The seat was set by someone a head taller than you.
Angle: setup, felt rather than explained. The most visual line here — one photograph carries it with no text at all.

The graveyard

Rejected on collision or exhaustion: anything containing "know what to do" (GymScan, Fitbod, Ladder), "no guesswork" (Fitbod), "train smarter" (Alpha Progression), "feel lost at the gym" (GymScan's subhead, and the project's own earlier draft), "your AI coach" (Freeletics), "think less" (Strong). Rejected on safety: every line built on protection, limits or supervision. Rejected as unmemorable: "the smart way to use gym equipment", "understand any machine", "your gym, decoded" — all three are descriptions pretending to be lines.

F

Pre-public truth gate

Nothing here blocks concept work. All of it blocks the first real user.

  1. Anchor hit rate measured on real nameplates, and the claim written to match the number rather than the ambition.
  2. Machine-level history and setup cards shipped, or removed from the page.
  3. Every claim re-mapped to the build at freeze, with the two recognition figures from earlier rounds retired — neither measures the shipped model or the production pipeline.
  4. Uncertainty behaviour audited per surface against the written rule.
  5. Safety and privacy pass: no protective claims anywhere, photo and pose claims scoped to exclude the cloud scanner, the Play-mandated sentence present exactly as the policy words it.
  6. Name and trademark cleared before a wordmark is drawn.
  7. Launcher label, release signing key, locale fallback decision, tester path.
  8. Competitor lines re-checked the week of publication — this field moved twice during four rounds of review.
