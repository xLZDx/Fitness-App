# User growth plan — 5 to 1,000 paying donors

**As of 2026-05-10.** Tactical playbook for the 0-to-1 user-acquisition
problem at the heart of every nonprofit. Strategy is in
`NONPROFIT_PLAN.md`; this doc is *only* about how to grow from 5–10
testnet users today to 1,000 paying donors sustaining the project. No
strategic re-framing — just channels, messaging, conversion mechanics,
and what unlocks the next stage.

---

## The shape of it

5 stages. Each has a clear unlock criterion to the next, a budget
that's mostly $0, and a measurement you can check weekly.

| Stage | Free users | Paying donors | Time | Cash spend | Primary channel |
|-------|-----------|---------------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **0 — Validation** | 0 → 10 | 0 | done | $0 | Personal network |
| **1 — First 100** | 10 → 100 | 0–5 | 30 days | $0 | Reddit + patient communities |
| **2 — First 1,000** | 100 → 1,000 | 5 → 50 | 90 days | $0–500 | ASO + content + referrals |
| **3 — First 10,000** | 1,000 → 10,000 | 50 → 500 | 6–9 months | $500–2k | Google Ad Grants + influencer + press |
| **4 — Self-sustaining** | 10,000+ | 1,000+ | 12–18 months | $2–10k/mo | All of the above + B2B |

The whole point is that **the project is self-sustaining at ~500 paying
donors out of 10,000 free users** — that's 5%, which is normal for
nonprofits with a strong mission. The leverage point is getting to
10,000 free users; the donation conversion takes care of itself.

---

## Stage 0 — Validation (where we are today)

**Goal:** confirm the app works end-to-end with real humans, not just
on the dev's emulator.

**Done if:** 5–10 friends/family have installed the APK, completed
onboarding, scanned a piece of equipment, completed at least one
workout, and given verbal feedback that the app didn't break.

**What you do this week:**
1. Send the APK + a 2-paragraph "what to test" email to 10 people in
   your personal network. Pick a mix:
   - 3 active gym-goers (the easy yes)
   - 3 post-injury people (the actual ICP)
   - 2 older adults (50+) (the ICP we don't usually catch)
   - 2 personal trainers / physiotherapists (clinical credibility
     check)
2. Set up a private Discord or WhatsApp group for them. Daily check
   for 14 days. Ask:
   - Did anything crash?
   - What was confusing?
   - What did you wish was there?
   - Would you pay $9.99/mo if it stayed free for users who couldn't?
3. Capture every bug + feature request in a Google Sheet. Triage
   weekly.

**Cost:** $0.

**Unlocks Stage 1:** at least 7 of 10 reach Day-7 retention (still
opening the app a week later) + zero blocking bugs.

## Stage 1 — First 100 free users (30 days)

**Goal:** prove the app survives outside the founder's network.

**Why this stage matters:** the friends-and-family stage gives you
inflated retention numbers because people are being polite. The first
100 strangers tell you whether the product actually clicks.

### Channel 1 — Reddit (highest ROI)

Reddit is the single best channel for nonprofit fitness with an
injury-aware angle. Five subreddits to engage in (don't spam — *help*):

| Subreddit | Members | Why we fit |
|---|---|---|
| `r/Fitness` | 13M | Default fitness conversation; we're useful when someone asks injury-question |
| `r/loseit` | 4M | Beginner-friendly, equipment-confused users |
| `r/orangetheory`, `r/CrossFit`, `r/powerlifting` | 200k–1M each | Specific equipment + injury overlap |
| `r/PhysicalTherapy` | 50k | DPTs + students; refer to our app for clients |
| `r/ChronicPain` | 200k | Underserved, mission-aligned |
| `r/ParkinsonsDisease`, `r/MultipleSclerosis` | 30–60k each | Adapted-fitness niches with no good app |
| `r/over50fitness`, `r/over60fitness` | 30–50k each | Older-adult ICP, very engaged |

**The exact play (don't shortcut this):**
- Spend 4 weeks reading + commenting helpfully *before* posting about
  the app. Build karma. Build trust. Reddit punishes drive-by promo.
- Pick one subreddit per week. Watch for posts like *"I have a knee
  injury and don't know what exercises are safe at the gym"*. Reply
  with a thoughtful answer (NOT promotion). At the end add: *"For
  what it's worth, I've been building a free app that filters
  exercises against your injury list. Happy to share if useful — DM
  me."*
- The DM-only-on-request rule keeps you out of moderator trouble.
- Once you have 50 free users, you can start posting in the
  recommendation threads ("what fitness app do you use?").

**Effort:** 30–45 minutes/day for 30 days.
**Yield:** 50–100 free installs from genuine Reddit engagement.

### Channel 2 — Patient-advocacy partnerships

These are the highest-impact installs because the users have the
strongest unmet need (injury / chronic condition / age-related).

Reach out to:
- **Local PT clinics** — ask if they'll mention the app to discharged
  patients. Give them a flyer + a personal-trainer-friendly QR code
  to the App Store / Play Store. Many clinic owners are happy to
  recommend a free + nonprofit option.
- **Foundation referral networks:**
  - American Chronic Pain Association
  - National MS Society
  - Arthritis Foundation
  - Parkinson's Foundation
  - American Heart Association (cardiac rehab grads)
- **Senior centers + YMCAs** — most have a wellness coordinator who
  refers older members to fitness apps; very few options exist that
  are designed for the older-adult use case.

**The pitch:** *"We're a nonprofit. The app filters out exercises
that conflict with the user's reported injuries. Free for your
patients. No data sales, no ads. We have an in-kind donation
acknowledgement letter for any clinic that promotes us."*

**Effort:** 5–10 cold emails/week + 2 in-person clinic visits/week.
**Yield:** 10–30 free installs/month from clinic referrals; each
typically refers more than once they trust us.

### Channel 3 — Personal-trainer pipeline (small but high-quality)

Trainers refer clients who use the app between sessions. Each trainer
might bring in 5–20 active users.

- LinkedIn search for "personal trainer" + "physiotherapist" in your
  city. Direct message: *"Building a nonprofit fitness app with
  injury-aware filtering reviewed by a DPT. Wondering if you'd want
  early access — happy to add features for what your clients need."*
- Trainers love being heard early. Many will post about the app to
  their own audience.
- Important: don't pay them. They're not affiliates. They recommend
  because the tool helps their clients. Affiliate-fee trainers tend
  to recommend whatever pays them most.

**Effort:** 5 trainer outreach messages/week.
**Yield:** 1–3 active trainer relationships/month, each bringing
5–20 users.

### Stage 1 unlock criteria

- 100+ free users with 30%+ Day-7 retention
- 10+ users have completed 5+ workouts (sticky users)
- 0 outstanding crash bugs
- App Store/Play Store rating ≥ 4.3 stars

If you hit those, Stage 2 is unlocked. If you don't, **don't move
on** — fix the retention problem first. Adding more users on top of
a leaky bucket just makes the leak bigger.

## Stage 2 — First 1,000 free users (60–90 days)

**Goal:** organic growth replaces hand-cranking.

### What changes vs Stage 1

- App Store Optimization (ASO) starts to matter. The app needs:
  - Clear icon + screenshots (5 screenshots minimum)
  - Title + subtitle keywords: "Fitness app · injury-aware ·
    personal trainer · free"
  - Description rewritten for SEO + conversion (emphasis on free +
    nonprofit + DPT-reviewed)
  - 30+ ratings 4-stars-or-higher (collected in Stage 1)
- Built-in referral mechanism: in-app "share with a friend" that
  generates a tracked link. Best to keep simple — no incentives, just
  social proof + ease.

### Channel 4 — Content marketing (long tail, compounds)

Blog + YouTube + TikTok. Even 1 post/week compounds because Google
indexes nonprofit health content favourably.

**Five topics that have a real chance of ranking:**

1. *"Safe exercises after a meniscus tear (rehab progressions)"*
2. *"How to use a leg press machine after lower back injury"*
3. *"Best gym exercises for people with osteoarthritis"*
4. *"Postpartum return-to-fitness: 8-week structured plan"*
5. *"Strength training with multiple sclerosis"*

These all rank for **buying-intent keywords** (people about to
search for an app). Each successful blog post drives 50–500
installs/month at zero ongoing cost.

**Effort:** 1 long-form post/week → ~4 posts/month. ~6 hours/post
including research + a quick video.
**Yield:** 50–200 organic installs/month after 3 months.

### Channel 5 — Press / earned media (the nonprofit boost)

Healthcare and nonprofit beats are more open than fitness beats.
Pitch:

- **Local TV health segments** — many local NBC/ABC affiliates have
  a "tech for good" segment. Pitch: *"Local nonprofit launches first
  fitness app for injury rehab — built by physiotherapists, free
  forever."*
- **Health-focused podcasts** — *Pain News Network*, *Arthritis
  Today*, *Chronically Honest* etc. They actively seek nonprofit
  guests.
- **Local newspaper / community magazines** — almost always positive
  coverage for a local-ish nonprofit.
- **Healthcare trade publications** — Health Affairs blog, MedCity
  News, FierceHealthcare. They cover digital health and especially
  digital-health nonprofits.

**Effort:** 1 pitch/week. Most journalists want a 1-paragraph pitch
+ 1 expert quote ready to go.
**Yield:** 1 placement per 5–10 pitches. Each placement drives
100–2,000 installs (highly variable; one viral local TV segment
can drive 5k).

### Channel 6 — Referral / sharing built-in

The product already has the bones for this — `ScheduledSession`
covers workout planning, so:

- "Send this workout to a friend" button → generates a deep link
- "Workout buddy" pre-stage feature (TX.3 in roadmap) once shipped
  unlocks gym-based virality

**Don't ship referral incentives.** Cash incentives bring fraud and
distort the user base. Genuine word-of-mouth from a strong product
is what scales nonprofits.

### Donation conversion starts here

Once we have 100+ active users, the in-app donation prompt becomes
ethical. **Where to ask:**

1. **After a "moment of value":**
   - First time the app filters out an exercise due to their injury
     (modal: *"This exercise was hidden because of your knee injury.
     We're a nonprofit — your donation keeps this safety filter free
     for everyone. Donate $9.99/month?"*)
   - 7-day streak (modal: *"You've worked out 7 days in a row. If
     this helps, a small monthly donation keeps us going.*")
   - 30-day milestone
2. **Welcome email at Day 3** — short, mission-focused, soft donation
   ask.
3. **Annual year-end campaign (December)** — biggest donation month.
   Email + in-app prompt.

**Critical rule:** never gate features behind donation prompts.
Mission credibility is fragile. Some users will donate; many won't;
that's fine.

**Conversion benchmarks:**
- Strong nonprofit fitness niche: 5–10% of monthly active users
  donate at least once over 12 months
- Of those: ~60% become recurring donors (the rest one-time)

**Year-1 math at 1,000 free users:** 5% × 1,000 × 60% recurring =
30 recurring + 20 one-time. **At our pricing: ~$3,000 / year.** Not
self-sustaining yet, but a clear signal that the model works.

### Stage 2 unlock criteria

- 1,000+ free users with 25%+ Day-30 retention
- 30+ recurring donors (3% conversion)
- App Store rating ≥ 4.5 stars
- At least one published article / podcast feature
- 4+ blog posts ranking on page 1 of Google for buying-intent
  searches

## Stage 3 — First 10,000 free users (6–9 months)

**Goal:** paid acquisition + partnerships scale us past where pure
organic growth tops out.

### Channel 7 — Google Ad Grants ($10k/month FREE for nonprofits)

Once 501(c)(3) is approved (or fiscal sponsor is active), apply for
Google Ad Grants:
- $10,000/month of free Google Ads spend
- Restrictions: keyword bids capped at $2.00, ads must serve
  nonprofit mission
- Realistic CPI on Android (cost per install): $1–3 in fitness category
- Effective reach: ~3,000–10,000 free installs/month at $0 cash cost

**This is the single highest-leverage channel in the entire plan.**

Apply:
1. Get 501(c)(3) status (or fiscal sponsor's status)
2. Apply at <https://www.google.com/grants>
3. Set up Google Ads account, agree to nonprofit-grant terms
4. Build keyword campaigns (start with "fitness app injury", "safe
   exercises after surgery", "free workout app no ads")
5. Conversion tracking via Firebase Analytics (already set up)

**Effort to set up:** 5 days. **Effort to maintain:** 2–3 hours/week
optimising bids + ad copy.

### Channel 8 — Influencer partnerships (organic, not paid)

Once we have user reviews + screenshots, micro-influencers are
willing to feature us *for free* if the mission resonates.

- Fitness YouTubers in adapted-fitness or rehab space (Bob & Brad,
  Squat University, the Athletic Therapist, etc) — often happy to
  shout out a nonprofit
- TikTok creators in chronic-pain / rehab niche
- Instagram physiotherapy accounts (DocLifts, MoveU, etc)

The pitch (modify per platform):

> *"Built a nonprofit fitness app with injury-aware filtering — free
> forever, no ads, no data sales. Reviewed by a DPT. Would love your
> honest take if it's useful for your audience.*"

**Effort:** 5 outreach DMs/week.
**Yield:** 1 successful collaboration/month. Each can drive
500–10,000 installs.

### Channel 9 — Corporate wellness B2B

Fortune 500 wellness programs often have CSR (corporate social
responsibility) budgets that fund nonprofits. Pitch the app as a
free benefit for employees with injuries.

- Reach out to HR / wellness leads at 50+ regional companies (insurance
  cos, hospitals, universities)
- Offer: free distribution + custom landing page + light reporting on
  usage. Their CSR team gets the credit; their employees get the app.

**Effort:** 2 hours/week B2B outreach.
**Yield:** Slow but high-LTV: 1 corporate partner brings 100–5,000
employees, of whom some convert to recurring donors organically.

### Channel 10 — Insurance partnerships (the moonshot)

The MK.4 feature in the roadmap was *"Insurance Premium Discount
Partnerships."* For a nonprofit this becomes much more realistic
because insurers are aligned on outcomes (fewer claims), not on
fitness-app revenue.

- Pitch to regional insurers + health plans: *"Our injury-aware app
  reduces re-injury risk in the post-PT population. Pilot it as part
  of your wellness benefit; the data on adherence + claim reduction
  is worth more than any direct revenue."*
- Best fit: regional Blue Cross Blue Shield plans, large self-insured
  employers (like Microsoft, Disney), insurance-tech companies (Lemonade,
  Oscar Health).

**Effort:** Multi-month BD work — start outreach in Stage 2, expect
12–18 months to the first signed partnership.
**Yield:** Once a partnership lands, can drive 5–50k users overnight
+ a recurring per-user-per-month revenue share.

### Stage 3 unlock criteria

- 10,000+ free users with 20%+ Day-30 retention
- 500+ recurring donors (5% conversion)
- App Store rating ≥ 4.6
- Year-1 revenue (from subscriptions + grants) ≥ $50k
- At least one corporate / insurance partnership in advanced talks

## Stage 4 — Self-sustaining at 1,000+ paying donors (12–18 months)

**Goal:** the project funds its own operations + growth without
founder savings or single-source dependency.

### What this looks like

| Metric | Stage 4 target |
|---|---|
| Free users | 25,000–50,000 |
| Paying donors | 1,000–2,500 |
| Conversion rate | 4–5% |
| Annual revenue (subscriptions) | $100k–$250k |
| Annual revenue (grants) | $50k–$200k |
| Annual revenue (corporate) | $25k–$100k |
| **Total ARR** | **$175k–$550k** |

This funds: 1 full-time exec director ($60–80k), 1 part-time
DPT contractor ($25k), 1 part-time engineer ($40–60k), legal +
infrastructure + marketing ($20–40k). Sustainable; not reliant on
heroic founder time.

### How to keep growing past 25,000

- All Stage 1–3 channels keep working at small marginal cost
- Add: paid acquisition beyond the Google Ad Grant (Meta + TikTok ads
  using grant + corporate sponsor money — $1–5/install in injury-aware
  niche)
- Year-2: NIH SBIR Phase II ($1M+) becomes plausible with year-1
  outcomes data
- Year-3: international expansion (UK + Canada have similar nonprofit
  structures + fewer competitors)

## Donation conversion mechanics — what to ship

The growth plan above gets users to the app. The conversion plan
below turns free users into recurring donors. Both need to ship for
either to matter.

### In-app donation prompts (5 screens to add)

1. **Welcome flow Day 3 modal** — *"You've used the app for 3 days.
   Here's how it stays free for users who can't afford to donate:
   recurring contributions from people like you. Want to help?"*
   - Two CTAs: "Donate $9.99/month" + "Maybe later"
   - Maybe-later flag persists; we don't ask again for 30 days
2. **First-injury-filter moment** — *"This exercise was hidden because
   of your reported knee injury. Our DPT reviewed every single one of
   200 exercises so you don't have to wonder. We're a 501(c)(3)
   nonprofit. Donate to keep this filter free for everyone?"*
3. **7-day streak celebration**
4. **30-day streak celebration**
5. **December year-end campaign** — limited-time, mission-led copy

### Email nurture sequence (6 emails over 60 days)

- Day 0 — welcome + mission intro
- Day 3 — first-week wins ("here's what users like you typically
  achieve")
- Day 7 — physio-reviewed safety story
- Day 14 — donation ask #1 (soft, mission-focused)
- Day 30 — your-first-month progress + donation ask #2 (harder)
- Day 60 — annual report excerpt + recurring donation pitch

Built into Firebase Cloud Functions — Stripe + email service (use
Resend or Mailgun, both have nonprofit free tiers).

### Conversion tracking

- Firebase Analytics already set up — add custom events for
  `donation_prompt_shown`, `donation_started`, `donation_completed`,
  `donation_declined`
- Cohort analysis: which prompt converts best? Which doesn't?
- Iterate copy monthly

### Tax-receipt automation

- Stripe webhook (already built) fires on payment
- Add a Cloud Function that emails the donor a Stripe-generated tax
  receipt within 1 hour
- Year-end aggregate letter (Jan 31) summing all donations for IRS
  purposes — required by US 501(c)(3) compliance

## What to spend money on, in order

Year-1 cash budget (~$5k from `NONPROFIT_PLAN.md`) prioritised for
maximum growth impact:

1. **D&O insurance** ($500–800) — required to incorporate, mandatory
2. **ATC catalog review** ($1,000) — needed before any public
   marketing claims about safety
3. **Stock content for catalog** ($500–2,000) — needed to look like a
   real product
4. **Apple developer + Google Play fees** ($25 + ~$0 with nonprofit
   waiver)
5. **Paid ad spend (Meta or TikTok), micro-budget** ($500–1,500 over
   the year, only after Stage 2) — only if Google Ad Grants is
   maxed

**Cash NOT to spend on (year 1):**
- Influencer payments — leverage in-kind donation pipeline instead
- Press release distribution services (PRWeb etc) — pitch journalists
  directly
- App Store featured-app placement — get there organically via ratings
- Conferences / speaking gigs — too expensive for the user-acquisition
  ROI at this stage

Every Monday, check:

| Metric | Target trajectory | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **Free users (cumulative)** | +30% MoM Stages 1–2; +15% MoM Stages 3–4 | Are growth channels firing? |
| **Day-7 retention** | ≥30% | Is the product actually useful? |
| **Day-30 retention** | ≥20% | Is it sticky? |
| **% completing 5+ workouts** | ≥10% | Are we converting installs to engaged users? |
| **App Store rating** | ≥4.5 ⭐ | Are users telling friends? |
| **Recurring donors** | +10% MoM after Stage 2 | Is conversion math working? |
| **Avg monthly donation amount** | ≥$8 | Are we attracting Sustainer-tier? |
| **Inbound press mentions** | 1+ /month after Stage 3 | Is the mission story landing? |

If any of these flatlines for 30 days, **pause growth spending and
fix the broken metric** before pushing more users in.

## What to NOT do

Five expensive mistakes nonprofits make in this stage:

1. **Don't gate features behind donations.** Mission credibility is
   fragile. Users smell cynicism instantly.
2. **Don't buy installs from low-quality networks.** App-install
   traffic from rewarded-ad networks looks like installs but doesn't
   retain. CPI of $0.30 is too good to be true.
3. **Don't pay Reddit influencers / KOLs.** Reddit moderators kill
   that overnight. Genuine helpfulness compounds; promotional
   shortcuts don't.
4. **Don't optimise too early for Stage 4 features** (insurance
   partnerships, B2B contracts) at the expense of Stage 1–2 user
   experience. Ship the basics insanely well first.
5. **Don't change the donation tier prices in the first 12 months.**
   Confuses early users + breaks Stripe subscription continuity.
   Set them correctly once, then leave them alone.
