### Consent gate rather than an explanatory banner

The proposal required **actual acceptance before camera access**.

- Place the gate in `runPhotoCaptureFlow` **before** `PhotoCaptureSheet.show` (`progress_photos_page.dart:184-215`), not inside the bottom sheet: the sheet initializes the camera and asks the OS for permission in `initState` (`photo_capture_sheet.dart:116-128`).
- **Keep the existing persistent `_PrivacyStrip`.** Replacing it with a one-off card was the reason the earlier R11f variant was rejected in the August 12 `DECISION_LOG.md` (Decision 4). Consent is **added alongside**, not instead of, the strip.
- Scope accepted consent **per account** using `progress_photos.consent.v1.<uid>`, consistent with photo AES-key (`photo_key_store.dart`) and directory (`photo_directory.dart`) scoping. Without an account, `InMemoryPhotoConsentStore` retains acceptance only for the current session.
- **Read failure → ask again (fail-closed)**. **Persistent write failure → acceptance lasts for the current session, but next launch asks again** via `PrefsPhotoConsentStore._acceptedInSession`.

### Copy and implementation assertions at the checkpoint

The six newly proposed `photosConsent*` localization keys were added to both ARB files. The original audit said its three claims were compared with code:

1. The photo was encrypted locally, not copied to a server, and this flow did not support upload/share.
2. Therefore there was **no backup**: reinstalling or losing the key would permanently lose photos. The earlier `progressphotosKeyMissing` message explained this only after the fact.
3. Camera frames were not persistently recorded: `camera_session.dart:443-461` used an in-memory `StreamController` rather than writing incoming frames to storage.

**These were historical statements scoped to R11f-1's code path**, not a fresh inspection of actual current storage/network behavior. Customer-facing privacy claims must be verified against current implementation before reuse.

## 4. Open review findings — none resolved at the source STOP

### MAJOR (review classification) — stale consent store over an async sheet gap

At `progress_photos_page.dart:193-207`, the `store` was resolved **before** showing the consent sheet; `await store?.accept()` ran **after** sheet completion. If the authenticated account changed while the sheet was open, the acceptance could be written to the **old** account's key.

**The source author disputed the reviewer's impact framing**, and explicitly required re-verification rather than treating either interpretation as fact:

- Reviewer concern: Alice's acceptance gets attributed to something presented as Bob's state.
- Source author's code-level interpretation: the write goes under **Alice's old key**, leaving Bob's consent gate closed. Under this interpretation the failure is **misattribution**, not a cross-account consent leak.
- The sheet was modal; an in-app account switch was unavailable while it was open. A background sign-out/expired auth session was the narrow candidate trigger.
- Potential remediation: resolve the store again after the sheet, under `context.mounted`, at the cost of reading provider/ref after an async gap — another lifecycle risk.

**No owner decision was made** about whether/how to remediate this MAJOR.

### MINOR — claimed account re-scoping lacked a test

`progress_photos_providers.dart:156-168` claimed sign-out re-closed the gate. The tests never exercised `authUserProvider` through logout/relogin. They instead replaced `photoConsentStoreProvider` wholesale and bypassed auth observation.

The same gap already affected `progressPhotosStoreProvider`. The source treated it as an **unverified assertion**, not a new regression caused solely by R11f-1.

### MINOR — gendered Russian consent CTA wording

The Russian `app_ru.arb` value at `photosConsentAccept` used a masculine short adjective meaning **"I agree"**. The original proposed a neutral verb equivalent to **"Allow"** or **"Open camera"** instead.
