The mechanic

Point the camera. Get the machine.

No stickers on the equipment. No setup by the gym. Nothing to install on the machine — it works on whatever is standing in front of you.

  1. 1

    Point

    Aim at the machine you mean. SPTR reads the words printed on it, and looks at the machine itself.

  2. 2

    Recognize

    It answers from a catalogue of 69 equipment types — in English and Russian, with the aliases people actually use.

  3. 3

    Do something with it

    1,887 exercises with steps and demonstration video, filtered against the injuries you tell it about.

Coming back

The second time should be easier than the first.

Knowing what a machine is called is useful once. What matters on the fourth visit is the part nobody remembers: where the seat was, and what you actually lifted.

  • What you lifted on this equipment last time, and when.
  • The setup you saved for it — seat, pad, pin, or whatever that machine actually has.
  • A suggested next weight, derived from how the last sessions went.

SPTR does not decide your settings. It shows you what you saved, and says when it saved it.

Evidence

It shows you what it read.

Many machines have their own name printed on them. When SPTR can use those words to make a match, it puts them on screen next to the answer — so checking it takes about a second, and you do not have to take its word for anything.

And when it is not sure, it says “Not sure — closest matches” and offers them, instead of picking one and sounding certain.

The honest part

Nothing recognises every machine. So it tells you when it doesn't.

An answer it cannot stand behind is worse than no answer. When a machine falls outside what SPTR knows, it says so and keeps the sighting instead of guessing.

It says it doesn't know

The machine gets a card of its own, marked “Not in the catalog yet”, with your photo of it.

It counts how often it comes up

Sightings of the same machine merge — “scanned 3 times” — so the gap has a size, not just a name.

The gaps set the queue

The equipment people actually stand in front of is what gets covered next. No promise about when.

Straight about the data

Where your pictures go, in plain words.

Progress photos stay on your phone

Encrypted on the device with a key held in the hardware keystore, and excluded from device backups.

Recognition photos are sent to be recognised

A machine photo goes to Google's Gemini through Firebase to be identified. That is not on-device, and the app says so too.

This page has no trackers

No analytics, no third-party scripts, no cookies. Nothing here reports back anywhere.

SPTR is being built in the open.

There is no company behind it and nothing to buy. It is one Android app, in English and Russian, made by people who got tired of guessing what a machine was for.
